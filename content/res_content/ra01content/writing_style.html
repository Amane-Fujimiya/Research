Purpose and scope

As it stands, this project aims to clarify theoretical assumptions, methodological limitations, and interpretive boundaries in Raman spectroscopy and related practices, particularly where theory, data processing, and modern ML pipelines interact (in which the final one is more of an after thought, but still good). The goal is not to replace existing theories or invalidate prior work, though there will be the case where we have to do it (reduce that to be honest), but to identify explicit assumptions, structural constraints, paradox between cross-interpretation, and downstream effectiveness of applications based on the theoretical core arguments. That said, while deliberately speaking we can use authoritative and flair, it is often seriously recommend, of Q1 journals to not use certain words and logical structures, even though it is sound, because it relies on rhetoric, emotional presentation, or simply attacks of people rather than people’s works. That said, a collection of journals accepting of Q1 all of those critiques are available, and in the event of us submitting to there, it should be not too worry. But simply speak, the margin of error should be done as recommended below, so to widen the gap.

Writing principles

The writing principle is as simple as to guide toward neutral tones and professional analysis, but not hindering thought and logical patterns, but only reformulation, presentation and so on.

General

Each theoretical framework, model, or processing step should be introduced together with: its domain of validity, its implicit assumptions, and known limitations. When present them and stating assumptions or theoretical framework, please use descriptive as possible, conditional phrasing (e.g. “valid under”, “commonly assumed”, “effective representation”) rather than evaluative or normative language. This is because words are weapons toward which in physics, the sensitive people procure resistant to their own works being criticised. I.e. so is to don’t ‘insult’ them academically. You can refer that to a list of neutral words and adjectives, and styling so on aside from the below section, using AIP Style Manual. There are also specific styling for example, instead of \(\alpha\)-particle, use \(\alpha\) particle without hyphen instead.

Specifically, for an example, Adjectives should be used sparingly and only when justified. Nature Physics specifically advises:

  • Avoid “very” - it adds syllables but not information
  • Avoid vague superlatives like “ultra” (e.g., “ultrashort”) - be specific about actual scales instead
  • Don’t overuse buzzwords like “quantum,” “nano,” or “bio” unless they’re central to your research
  • Avoid empty phrases like “Holy Grail” or “opening new avenues” unless historically so, but otherwise don’t.

That said, stronger journals might want direct criticism and so on, and over-soften anything might prove disastrous. In that case, it is then recommended for you and later development to choose between make strong claims about contradictions (just frame them as “theoretical inconsistencies” not “errors”) or not, Point out practical failures (link them to reproducibility, not incompetence) or not, and Propose reforms (as “refinements” or “extensions” rather than replacements) or not, depends on the severity of the problem and parties involved.

No personal attribution of fault

Structural issues are framed as field-level practices, not individual errors or motivations. Historical sections as specification, not judgement, thus when writing historical developments, the contents are and should be described in terms of:

  • What was assumed of the old theory, and their assumptions, stated explicitly of neutral and descriptive way,
  • What was explained, using the author’s or the theory’s own voice (or explaination),
  • and what was left outside scope at the time. Hence, inherent at-that-time limitation and falsifiable concepts.

All of which is to reduce attribution to certain people’s lifeline and so on, since our topic is rather dangerous to tread. However, in certain cases, you can, because of our venue of submission to be very receptive to active criticism and not flair of softend notice. #### On critique and limitations

Critiques should be framed as constraints on use, not declarations of incorrectness. When possible, link theoretical issues to practical consequences (e.g. interpretability, reproducibility, ML leakage), not to philosophical disputes alone. We explicitly acknowledge that many issues discussed are not resolved within this paper, as it is identifying problems and proposing a rather hasty fashion of a reformation, or where to focus, where to specify, not resolving themselves. This is important enough, very much so.

When interfacing with ML or data-driven methods, clearly distinguish between physical signal, preprocessing artifacts (baseline correction reveals granular of error, or for example, the deviation on harmonic analysis, etc), and model-induced structure. Avoid language that implies physical meaning where identifiability is unclear, so is to reduce claims without metric, and to remove resistance from addressing them in the wrong manner.

What this paper does not attempt

The following list the to-do and not to-do:

To do Not to do
Highlight paradox, identify potential misconception and contractions only in the sense of theoretical mismatch (since every theory has its purposes). It does not provide a unified theory of Raman spectroscopy.
All of them are competing with extension or reformulation, but the old foundational theories still works, and should not include foundational, since the topic would be stretched out. It does not adjudicate between competing foundational interpretations.
Historically, of the ‘present time’ in history, those are the best we have. So use neutral tone and specify scope instead of blaming (it is true, but please do it in neutral tone and reduce friction instead). It does not assign blame for historical or contemporary practices.

Collaborative expectations

Disagreements are expected and documented explicitly, so is to be resolved; even disagreement of technicality between different team members is good but must be available for resolution closure. If a claim feels “too strong”, the first response should be to add conditions, not to remove the claim. If a section risks overreach, we prefer narrowing scope over dilution. The goal overall is to make less of unwanted friction between us and the potential reviewer, but still keeps the legitimacy of the content. That is why we should also attribute as much as citation, but key citation and key placement of them in the document together for maximum effect.
